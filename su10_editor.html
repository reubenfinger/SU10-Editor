Note# is what this editor sends and listens for — it must match the SU10’s own table (EDIT › Note#, or UTILITY › MIDI › NoteTbInit for the default C1–B4). Use Learn: click it, then press the pad on the SU10 and its note is picked up. As on the unit, giving a note to one pad sets any other pad using it to Off.
Without a dump loaded, LpMode, SdMode, PadVol and AltGrp are just notes. Once a bulk dump is open in SU10 Memory, they show the unit’s real settings, and any change here goes into the rebuilt dump. Names and Memo always stay in this editor only.

Back up: SU10 → computer

  1. Click Start capture.
  2. On the SU10: SHIFT+UTILITY › Bulk › Dump Send? (set Intrvl to Short first) › ENTER › ENTER.
  3. The SU10 stops three times at Next OK?. Press ENTER each time. Capture stays armed.
  4. When the SU10 says Complete, click Stop & save .syx.

Timing (from susend)

Restoring replaces everything on the SU10. If data stops for 30 seconds during a transfer, the unit shows Time Out! and its memory is lost, so don’t abort halfway unless you have to. Timing runs in a background worker, so changing tabs shouldn’t stall it, but keep the computer awake.

Receive a sample dump

  1. Connect SU10 MIDI OUT → computer IN (and computer OUT → SU10 IN for the handshake).
  2. On the SU10: SHIFT+UTILITY › MSDS, pick a pad, press ENTER.
  3. For a stereo sample the SU10 sends the left side, then asks Send R OK?. Press ENTER and both halves are joined here.

About MSDS on the SU10

The SU10 can send samples by MIDI Sample Dump Standard but can’t receive them (manual p.62 and p.70). The manual says sample IDs run 1–48, but your unit sends A1 = 0 … D12 = 47; the editor uses what the unit sends.

The SU10 sends the sample forward, from StPnt to EdPnt. The rate it reports is the one the sample was recorded at (recording grade × recording pitch). It doesn't change when you switch the unit’s grade, which only shifts the pitch on playback. Your pad settings stay on the SU10. To get samples into the unit, record them through LINE IN (see Sample Prep), or restore a bulk dump.

Captured samples are kept only until you close this page. Save the WAVs you want to keep.

Grade rates & what's known about the format

The playback rate for each grade isn't in the dump. Long (code 2) = 20,050 Hz, from your SU10’s own MSDS header (your by-ear check gave 19,986 Hz, 5.5¢ away). High = 2 × Long. Standard = 29,090 Hz and Extra Long = 14,550 Hz, both from MSDS of samples recorded at those grades. An MSDS capture sets the current grade's rate exactly.

Decoded from your dump:

  • The dump is 50 sections of 8 KB (Yamaha 43h, model 36h), packed 7 data bits per MIDI byte. Each block's checksum is −(block number + the block's unpacked bytes), 7-bit.
  • Section 0 is 48 × 42-byte sample records, the system settings at 7E0h, and the note→pad table at 7FCh. Section 1 is song memory. Sections 2–49 are the 384 KB of wave memory.
  • Audio is 4-bit IMA-ADPCM, low nibble first. The decoder state is stored per sample.

Not yet confirmed: what an empty pad looks like, how stereo samples are stored, and which grade each grade code means. Bulk receive always clears the whole unit first, so a full image is always sent.

Add to SU10 Memory

To record: on the SU10 go to REC, set Grade / Format / Input = Line and RecTrg = 1–2, press PLAY to arm, then click Play into SU10 LINE IN. The editor waits about 0.5 s and then plays the prepared sound out of this computer’s audio output. Long = 20,050 Hz comes from your SU10’s own sample dump, and High is twice that. Standard = 29,090 Hz and Extra Long = 14,550 Hz were measured from your unit’s own sample dumps.
